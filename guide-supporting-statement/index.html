A practical guide

How to write a supporting statement that answers the actual criteria

Some application forms do not want a longer cover letter. They want you to answer a list. The statement works when every criterion can be found, with an example, inside the limit they set.

InterviewAlpha guide · 11 minutes

A supporting statement is a piece of writing some employers ask for on the application form. You may see it called a personal statement, a statement of suitability, or further information. It is common where a public body, a university, or a charity scores candidates against a published list, and it also appears on other forms that say "address the criteria" rather than "attach a CV and we will infer the rest." The name on the form matters less than the instruction under it.

Readers of these forms are often checking coverage. Did you answer this line? Is there an example, not a promise? Did you stay inside the limit? A warm paragraph about your values can be pleasant and still fail, because it never reached the third criterion. This guide is a way to make a statement that can be marked against the list you were given.

Keep thisA supporting statement is a set of answers. Cover the criteria they wrote, in the space they gave you.

01

What the form is asking

A cover letter introduces you and points at the role. A CV lists the jobs. A supporting statement is closer to an exam script: the questions are the criteria, and the marks sit on whether each one is evidenced. The National Careers Service, the government's careers advice service in England, describes skills questions on application forms, including examples from work or volunteering, and says to match your words to the advert. Its STAR guidance puts situation, task, action, and result to work in a further information section as well as in an interview. That is the right weight of structure. You are not writing a memoir of the job.

If the form gives you one box for the whole statement, you still organise it by criterion. If it gives you a box per criterion, do not pour your entire career into the first box and then write "as above" in the rest. Each box is a question. A blank box is a criterion you did not answer.

Keep a copy of what you submit. The National Careers Service advises that, partly so you can prepare if you are interviewed on it. You will be. Anything in the statement is fair material for a follow-up question. If you would not say it aloud, do not leave it in the box. Preparing when you only have one evening assumes you can find those examples again. A statement you cannot remember is a poor bargain.

InterviewAlpha tip If they invite you, the next action is to prepare from that statement. Put the interview on the Today list so the examples you wrote are the ones you revise.

02

Read the criteria before you write

Print the list, or copy it into a note, before you open a blank page. Mark three things on each line:

  • Essential or desirable. Essentials need an example. Desirables need an example if you have one, and a short honest limit if you do not. The guide on applying without every requirement is how you decide whether a missing essential is a reason to skip the role entirely.
  • The whole statement, or one box. A limit of a few hundred words for everything is a different writing problem from a limit on each answer. Read the instruction above the box, not a limit you remember from another employer.
  • What kind of proof they asked for. "Experience of" wants a scene. "Ability to" wants what you did. "Knowledge of" can be a course plus where you used it, not a definition you copied from a textbook. "Commitment to" still needs a scene. A sentence of belief will not show the commitment.

Watch for criteria that overlap. "Working with non-technical colleagues" and "explaining analysis clearly" can share a story. They should not share a paragraph that answers neither. Split the actions. One scene can feed two paragraphs if each paragraph earns a different point.

Also watch for criteria that are conditions in disguise: a qualification, a licence, the right to work, a location. Those belong in the correct fields on the form. Do not spend half the statement proving a fact the form has already asked for, and do not hide a missing condition inside pleasant prose.

03

A coverage table

The table below is an illustrative plan for a senior data analyst application at a city transport office. The criteria are the sort a public employer or a university professional-services team might publish. Your list will differ. The columns should not. If you cannot fill "evidence" for an essential, you are not ready to write that paragraph, and you may not be ready to apply.

Illustrative coverage for one application
Criterion Evidence you will use Where it goes
Experience analysing data to improve a service At Harbour Transit, the missed-connection report counted rows that included cancelled trips. You rebuilt the definition with the operations lead and the weekly meeting stopped arguing about the total. First paragraph. The result is the meeting, not a percentage you did not calculate.
Working with colleagues who are not analysts You sat with two duty managers and rewrote the column names in their words. They could pull the report without you. Second paragraph. Your action is the rewrite, not "collaborated widely".
Writing that a non-specialist can use A one-page note on a timetable change, which the press office used without a further draft. Third paragraph. Name the reader and what they did with the writing.
Desirable: SQL or a similar tool You wrote the queries. You have not used their particular warehouse. A short paragraph. Name the tool you use. Do not claim theirs.

Notice what is absent. There is no paragraph reserved for "I have always loved transport" and none for a retelling of the CV from the first job forward. Motivation can have two sentences at the end if the form asks why this role. It does not get the space the essentials need.

Build the same three columns for your advert before you draft a sentence. Evidence comes from scenes you could point to. If the scene has no number, write the change in words. Achievements when the job has no obvious numbers is the method for finding those scenes without inventing a figure.

InterviewAlpha tip The examples in the statement and the bullets on the CV need to be the same version. Link that version to this application before you submit the form.

04

A paragraph planner

One essential criterion, one paragraph, unless the form has already merged two ideas into a single line. Use the same shape each time so a tired reader can find the answer:

  1. Name the criterion in the first line if the box is not already labelled. "In my current role I analyse service data that operations staff use to change a timetable."
  2. One situation, in a sentence. Where, and what was wrong or unfinished.
  3. The action that was yours. Verbs a teammate would recognise. Not "was involved in".
  4. The result you can defend. What was different afterwards. Stop. Do not add a second story to fill the line.

Here is that shape, written out for the first criterion above. It is illustrative.

A paragraph that answers the lineI analyse data to change a service, not only to describe it. At Harbour Transit the missed-connection report treated cancelled trips as if they had run, so the weekly total could not be trusted. I rebuilt the definition with the operations lead, documented it beside the query, and used that version in the Monday pack. The meeting stopped opening with an argument about the number and started on which connections to change.

A paragraph that misses itI am a passionate analyst and a strong communicator who thrives in fast-paced environments. I would bring my stakeholder skills and my love of public service to this exciting opportunity.

The second paragraph could be pasted under any criterion and would answer none of them. Cut that kind of sentence on sight, even if it sounds like the opening you were taught to write.

If two criteria need the same project, tell the project once, in the paragraph that needs the situation, and in the second paragraph start from the action that criterion cares about. "As in the example above, I also..." is acceptable when the form is tight. It is not acceptable as the whole answer.

Write in the first person. "I rebuilt the definition" is clearer than "the candidate" or "one was responsible for". Match names, dates, and tools to the CV. A statement that promotes you to a title you did not hold will be read next to the employment history. The clash is the thing they remember.

05

Stay inside the limit you were given

There is no correct length for a supporting statement in general. There is the limit on this form. If they give none, cover each essential in a short paragraph, give desirables less space, and stop when the list is answered. Do not hunt for a famous word count from a different employer and then pad or slash to hit it.

When they do give a limit, treat it as a budget before you draft. The figures in this section are an illustration of splitting one stated limit. They are not a rule for the next form you open.

Suppose the box says 400 words for the whole statement, and you have four essentials plus one desirable and a line on why this role. A workable split is about 80 words for each essential, about 40 for the desirable, and about 40 for why this role. That uses the 400. Write the essentials first. If you are over, cut the scene-setting and the adjectives, not the result. If you are far under, you have probably described a duty in a clause and called it an example. Go back to the coverage table rather than adding enthusiasm.

Count the words in the text you will paste, including headings you type into the box. A draft in a note is often shorter or longer than the field that accepts it. If the form counts for you, believe the form. If it does not, count the pasted text. Leave a little room, perhaps a sentence, so a last clarification fits. Do not leave a fifth of the limit empty out of caution, and do not go over because the last story is your favourite.

Per-criterion limits are simpler. Answer that line fully, then stop, even if you have another story. A second story that pushes you over can cost you the first.

06

Worksheet: coverage and word limit

Copy this or print it. Complete the lines for this application only. Then use the checks before you submit.

Role and limit

The job title, and the word limit as written on the form. Whole box, or per criterion.

Essential criteria and the example for each
Desirables I can evidence, and any I will state as a limit
Word budget

How many words for each essential, each desirable, and a close, adding up to the limit. Skip this if the form sets no limit.

  1. Every essential criterion has its own example, in a paragraph or a labelled box.
  2. Desirables are covered or honestly limited. None are invented.
  3. I have not retold the whole CV, and I have not led with a general claim about my personality.
  4. Names, dates, and tools match the CV I am sending with this form.
  5. The word count is the count of the text in the box, and it is inside the limit.
  6. I could talk through every example in an interview without notes I should not have.
  7. The employer's name, the role title, and the spelling have been checked on this draft, not on an older one.
  8. I have saved a copy with the application.

07

Frequently asked questions

Is a supporting statement the same as a cover letter?

No. A cover letter introduces you. A supporting statement answers criteria. Some employers want both. Follow the form in front of you. If it only asks for a CV and a short note, do not send a criteria essay they did not request.

Can I reuse a statement for the next job?

You can reuse a scene. You should not reuse the statement. The criteria change, and a paragraph that answered a previous list will miss this one. Run the coverage table again. It is faster than discovering the miss after a rejection you cannot interpret.

What if I do not meet one criterion?

If it is a genuine barrier, such as a licence or a right to work the employer will not support, do not bury it in the statement. Decide whether to skip, using the apply, clarify, or skip test. If it is desirable, or a close miss on the work, write the nearest true example and the limit in one or two sentences. Do not leave an essential box empty if the form expects an answer, and do not fill it with a claim you cannot discuss.

Should I use headings inside the box?

Use headings when the form gives you one box and several criteria. Short headings that repeat their wording help a reader score the page. Skip decorative headings, and skip headings if they have already given you a separate box per line.
