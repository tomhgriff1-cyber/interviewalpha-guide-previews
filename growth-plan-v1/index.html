A. Google Search ads test

What it is for: put InterviewAlpha in front of people already looking for a job tracker. Search ads are plain text results shown for selected searches. You pay when someone clicks.

How long until results: a few days for first clicks; 14 days for this initial learning test. Do not expect a certain number of sign-ups.

Effort: about 60 to 90 minutes to set up with help, then 15 minutes on days 3, 7 and 14. Dev must finish measurement and any free-tool page before switching on.

Cost: recommended £100 total for 14 days. Start within the requested £50 to £100 test range only when Tom approves this exact amount. £150 is a soft overall ceiling for this test, not permission to spend £150 automatically. £7.14 a day gives £99.96 over 14 days. Google can spend unevenly on individual days, so check the actual campaign cost and pause it when it reaches £100.

What success looks like: relevant UK search terms, at least one attributable Free sign-up, and clear evidence about which phrase brought it. No sign-ups is a reason to stop and learn, not to increase the budget automatically.

Negative keywords

Negative keywords tell Google which searches not to show the ad for. Start with this list and inspect actual search terms during the test:

recruiter software, employer, applicant tracking system, ATS software, HR software, hiring software, staffing, employee, time tracking, employee tracker, work tracker, delivery, job centre, NHS jobs login, track my NHS application, salary tracker, CRM, jobs hiring, vacancies, Indeed login, LinkedIn login, Teal login, Huntr login, AI apply, auto apply, autofill, application writer, cover letter writer, free CV writer, torrent, crack.

These are not claims about those tools or people. They simply prevent irrelevant searches. Do not block every use of "free": Free is part of the product. "ATS" should be negative because applicants searching for portal statuses are usually not looking for recruiter software, but the ad may still answer candidate tracker phrases.

What Dev needs to do, and the privacy choice

  • Preserve only the agreed UTM source, medium, campaign and content values from landing page to sign-up and save them with the account. No need to add a Google Ads tag for this plan.
  • Add source and campaign to the Monday Free sign-up count. Report clicks and spend from Google Ads separately and put them in the simple sheet. Do not send customer emails or user-level data to Google.
  • Agree a fixed, short retention period for these campaign-source fields, and update the privacy notice if needed. Campaign source can still be data linked to an account, so this is lower data collection, not guaranteed anonymous measurement.
  • Do not add the Google Ads conversion tag in this version. Google's own consent reference describes advertising cookies, click IDs and data sent to Google when its tags operate with consent. A tag would therefore require a separate privacy review, privacy.md change and appropriate cookie/ad consent setup. Enhanced conversions, which send hashed user-provided data, are explicitly out of scope.
  • Use UTM naming only, no Google click ID or individual advertising profile. Disable any automatic cross-site advertising tags or imported conversions if they get suggested during account setup.

The two-week decision rule

  • Day 3: check UK presence, search terms and live links. Add negatives for obviously irrelevant queries. Do not change three things at once.
  • Day 7: check spend, clicks and attributable Free sign-ups. Stop irrelevant or clearly employer-focused queries. Keep the daily cap unless Tom approves a change. Do not boost a keyword only because it got clicks.
  • Day 14 or £100 spent, whichever comes first: pause before deciding.
  • Keep: one or more relevant attributable Free sign-ups and search terms that match the product. Continue only with a separately approved budget, holding the same wording and keywords for another comparable test.
  • Change: relevant searches and clicks but no sign-ups. Change one landing-page message or call to action and retest only after Tom approves. First check the account creation flow and source attribution; the result may be measurement failure rather than marketing failure.
  • Stop: irrelevant queries, unexpected cost with no useful learning, or no sign-ups after the £100 learning test unless Tom deliberately approves a changed landing page. No sign-ups plus very few clicks means the test is inconclusive, not that marketing or the product has failed.
  • Paid upgrades: observe them in later Monday reports but do not require one within two weeks. The test is too small for that conclusion.

B. More useful guides

What it is for: help someone solve a job-search problem even if they do not sign up today. Search engines can then show that guide when someone asks the same thing. Each guide should be genuinely useful first and include a relevant demo or sign-up link second.

How long until results: expect about 2 to 3 months before judging a new guide, and sometimes longer. Publishing does not create an immediate audience. Google Search Console will show whether the pages are appearing in search; Dev can install and verify it if not already present, with a privacy check.

Effort: about 3 to 5 hours for one properly researched, reviewed guide with screenshots, plus a light quarterly check. Do not publish all 15 at once.

Cost: £0 publishing cost unless Tom approves outside help.

What success looks like: search impressions and visits after several weeks, clicks to /demo or /signup, then Free sign-ups with the guide as source. One useful visit is more valuable than lots of irrelevant traffic.
