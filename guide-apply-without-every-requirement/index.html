A practical guide

Should you apply if you do not meet every requirement?

A job advert mixes conditions, the work itself, and a wish list. Meeting every line is a different question from being able to do the job. Here is how to tell them apart.

InterviewAlpha guide · 12 minutes

You read a software advert and tick off the lines you match. Python, yes. The years of experience, almost. A tool you have only used in a side project, no. A sentence about sponsorship, unclear. The usual advice then splits into two bad options: apply to everything, or wait until you match the page from top to bottom. Neither is a decision. It is a mood.

A CV or resume can show that you have done similar work. It cannot remove a barrier the law, a regulator, or the employer has set as a condition. This guide separates those lines, then uses one tech advert to practise the split. The aim is a smaller set of applications you can defend, not a trick for slipping past the list.

Keep thisA line on an advert is a barrier, evidence you can show, or a wish. Those are different decisions: skip, apply, or ask.

01

Three kinds of line

Employers do not use one vocabulary. "Must", "required", "essential", "you will", "nice to have", and a bare bullet can all appear in the same advert. Read the meaning, not only the heading. Sort each line into one of three piles before you decide.

  1. A condition. Something that stops you doing the job even if the team likes you. The right to work, when the employer will not sponsor. A licence or registration the law requires for that title. A security clearance the contract says you must already hold. A place you would have to be, on days you cannot be there.
  2. The core of the work. The skills the role is hired to use most weeks. For a backend engineer, that might be building and running a service. For a data analyst, analysing a real dataset and explaining it. For a product manager, shaping a problem with engineers and with the people who use the product. You do not need every tool. You do need evidence that you have done this kind of work.
  3. A desirable extra. Tools, sectors, and years that would help and are not the job itself. "Familiarity with", "bonus", "ideally", and a long stack of technologies usually live here. A degree that is not legally required for the title often lives here too, unless the advert makes it a condition of the contract.

The National Careers Service, the government's careers advice service in England, tells you to read the essential criteria on the advert and tailor the CV to them. CareerOneStop, a careers site sponsored by the US Department of Labor, suggests listing each requirement and writing your qualification beside it, then planning how you will address a gap. That pairing is the whole method. A gap you can explain with adjacent work is not the same as a gap where the work is missing.

Harvard Business School, a university business school, summarises its Hidden Workers research: restrictive hiring filters can exclude capable candidates. The 2021 study surveyed workers and executives in the US, UK, and Germany. It is a warning about filters that are tighter than the work. It is not permission to ignore a real condition, and it is not a measurement of your chances on a particular advert.

02

Genuine barriers

Skip a role when a condition is false for you and the advert does not offer a way through. Applying in the hope that nobody will notice wastes the application, and a false answer on a form is worse than a skip. Forms often ask about work authorisation, location, and qualifications in their own fields. Those answers need to match the truth, whatever you do with the CV.

Right to work and sponsorship

If the advert says they cannot offer sponsorship, and you need a new visa or a new right to work for that job, skip it. If the advert says sponsorship is available, the condition is different: you can apply and answer the question as asked. If the advert is silent, you may clarify, in one sentence, rather than guessing the policy or hiding the fact on the form. Do not invent a visa category or assume a rule from another country applies. Read the line they wrote.

Licences, clearances, and legal titles

Some jobs cannot be done without a current licence or registration. Software roles rarely work that way. A role that says you must already hold a specific clearance, or a professional licence the law requires, is a barrier if you do not hold it and they will not help you get it. "Willing to undergo a background check" is not the same line. A check you can complete is a process. A clearance you must bring on day one is a condition.

Place and hours

A role that is on site in a city you cannot reach, on days you cannot work, is a condition. A preference for hybrid, written as "ideally in the office two days", is not. If you could do the stated pattern, do not talk yourself out of the role because the pattern is inconvenient. If you could not, do not apply and hope they will redesign the job.

03

Desirable experience, and the close miss

Years of experience are often a proxy for having done the work. "Five years of Python" is trying to say "you have shipped with it, more than once, and you have seen it break." If you have done that in three years, you can apply and show the work. If you completed a course last month and have not built anything someone relied on, the years are not the real gap. The work is.

A close miss means you can point at the core of the job in your own history. You have built the kind of system, run the kind of analysis, or led the kind of decision, even if the tool or the sector differs. An interest in learning the job from the beginning is not a close miss. Keenness is attractive beside evidence. It does not replace it.

Tools date. A data analyst who has used one SQL warehouse well can often learn another. Say the one you have used, and do not add the one you have not. A product manager who has shipped in one industry can apply to another when the advert's core is the product work, not a licence to practise in that industry. A software engineer who has never worked on the thing the team exists to do, such as a production payments service when the whole role is payments, should treat that as the core, not as a nice-to-have stacked at the bottom.

When you apply with a gap, give it one sentence, then the evidence. Do not apologise for the rest of the page.

Leave this outI know I do not meet every requirement, but I am a fast learner and I would love the chance to prove myself.

Write this, if it is trueI have not run Kubernetes in production. I have shipped the service in Docker, written the health checks, and paired with the platform team on the last two releases.

Illustrative wording. Use it only where the second sentence is something you have done. If you cannot write the second sentence, this is a skip or a conversation with the contact on the advert, not a paragraph of enthusiasm.

05

Apply, clarify, or skip

Apply

Apply when every condition is true, or the employer has offered a route through it, and you can show the core of the work in sentences a stranger can check. Desirable lines can be missing. Say so once if the gap is obvious, then return to the evidence. Tailor the CV to the core, using the words they use where those words are true. The guide to screening software covers how to keep that evidence in a file a form can read. It does not ask you to paste the advert into the document.

Clarify

Clarify when one line is ambiguous and the rest of the role is a close match. Is the clearance required before you apply, or after an offer? Is the degree a preference or a rule of the contract? Is the language essential, or an example of the kind of language? Write to the contact on the advert, if there is one, in a few lines: the role, the line you are asking about, and the fact that lets them answer. If there is no contact, one plain sentence in the application is enough. Do not send a defence of your whole career, and do not ask them to confirm that you are good enough. Ask the question the advert left open.

Skip

Skip when a condition is false and there is no route through it. Skip when you cannot evidence the core, however much you like the product. Skip when the level is a different job: a first role dressed as a staff engineer post, or a staff post you are treating as a way in. Skipping is part of a serious search. If almost every advert becomes a skip, the problem may be the titles you are reading, which is the finding stage in where a job search gets stuck.

If the form asks you to address criteria in a statement, do not hide a gap in a general paragraph about motivation. Give each essential criterion an example or a short, honest limit. Writing a supporting statement shows how to cover the list. Showing achievements without invented numbers is how you fill the lines you do meet.

When you apply or clarify, note the gap you disclosed and the next action, such as waiting or sending the one question. InterviewAlpha can keep that application, the CV version you used, and the next action together, which matters when you later compare which version is getting interviews.

06

Checklist: apply, clarify, or skip

Use this on one advert before you write the application. Copy it, or print the worksheet and mark it by hand.

  1. I have sorted every line into a condition, the core of the work, or a desirable extra.
  2. Every condition is true for me, or the advert offers a route I can honestly use.
  3. I can point to work I have done that matches the core, not only to a willingness to learn it.
  4. Any missing desirable is either left out or named in one sentence followed by real evidence.
  5. I have not added a tool, a qualification, or a year count I cannot discuss in an interview.
  6. If a line is ambiguous, I know the single question I will ask, and who I will ask.
  7. If I am skipping, I can say which condition or which missing core made it a skip.
  8. The CV and the form tell the same story, including eligibility answers.
  9. This application is for a level I can defend, not a title I hope they will reinterpret.
  10. I know the next action after I send it: wait, clarify, or move on.

07

Frequently asked questions

What if the advert marks every line as required?

Treat legal, licence, clearance, sponsorship, and on-site conditions as barriers. Treat the rest by whether you can show the work. A heading that says "requirements" does not turn a nice-to-have tool into a law. It also does not turn a missing core skill into a minor gap.

Should I apply when I match most of the list?

There is no percentage that makes an application sensible. Two missing desirable tools can still be an apply. One false condition, or a core you have never done, is a skip even if you match everything else. Count the kind of line, not the number of bullets.

Will I be rejected for a missing nice-to-have?

You cannot know. Some reviewers treat the whole list as a filter. Some do not. Harvard Business School's Hidden Workers summary describes how tight filters can screen out people who could do the work. Your part is to make the core obvious and not to fake the extra. Their part is the decision.

Should I say I am happy to learn the missing skill?

Yes, in a clause, after evidence of the nearest work you have done. No, as the main argument. "I am a fast learner" without an example is a line anyone can write.
