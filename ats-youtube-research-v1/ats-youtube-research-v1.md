# Getting a CV past screening software: what actually matters

*InterviewAlpha ATS YouTube research v1 | 6 November 2026 | Research only*

**20 videos | 20 usable transcripts | about 38,930 transcript words including headings | 18 independent sources**

Three TubeAlfred searches were used: “how to optimise for CV screening tools”, “ATS CV tips UK” and “beat applicant tracking system resume”. The set contains 5 UK, 11 US, 2 Canadian, 1 Australian and 1 global-market video. View counts are snapshots, not evidence.

> **How to read the verdicts:** Supported means primary or strong official evidence backs the advice. Mixed means the answer depends on the setup or the claim is too broad. Myth means unsupported or misleading as stated. Consensus counts show how many of the 20 videos mention an idea, not whether the idea is true.

## 10 things that actually matter

### 1. Tailor the CV to each job, using honest language from the advert
**Video consensus: 20/20** [##########]
Every video says to align content with the specific job. The useful version is selective editing, not rewriting everything or inventing experience.
**Evidence verdict: Supported.** Across all 20. The National Careers Service also recommends tailoring to the job and highlighting requested skills.

### 2. Use a simple, single-column layout with real text
**Video consensus: 17/20** [########--]
Keep important facts out of images, icons, skill bars and complex design elements. Simple does not mean careless. Clear hierarchy and white space still matter.
**Evidence verdict: Supported.** Greenhouse specifically lists columns, tables, graphics, text boxes, headers and footers as potential parsing problems.

### 3. Show relevant achievements, not a list of duties
**Video consensus: 16/20** [########--]
Choose the experience that proves fit for this job. Replace vague responsibilities with what changed because of your work.
**Evidence verdict: Supported.** Strong creator agreement. This is about human review as well as matching. No format can compensate for weak evidence.

### 4. Give outcomes and numbers you can verify
**Video consensus: 13/20** [######----]
Use scale, time saved, accuracy, speed or service outcomes where genuinely available. Do not manufacture a metric for every bullet.
**Evidence verdict: Supported.** Teal usefully says the first bullet under each role matters most. Numbers are evidence, not an ATS spell.

### 5. Use familiar section names
**Video consensus: 11/20** [######----]
Work experience, education and skills are safer than clever labels. Put the material the employer wants where a person and parser expect it.
**Evidence verdict: Supported.** Greenhouse warns that unclear sections and inconsistent formats can cause partial parsing.

### 6. Keep dates, employers and job titles clear and consistent
**Video consensus: 9/20** [####------]
Use a repeatable order, readable month and year dates, and recognisable job titles. Explain an unusual internal title honestly rather than inflating it.
**Evidence verdict: Supported.** Workday says parsing varies with format and word order. Greenhouse says incomplete titles can cause incomplete results.

### 7. PDF and Word can both work. Follow the application instructions
**Video consensus: 10/20** [#####-----]
Only 10 videos commit to file advice. Four favour PDF, two favour Word and four accept both or say to follow the advert. Never use an image-only file.
**Evidence verdict: Mixed.** There is no universal winner. Greenhouse, Taleo and Lever accept text-based PDF and DOCX. Workday features differ, so check each form.

### 8. Check the text extraction and the autofilled application
**Video consensus: 4/20** [##--------]
Three videos suggest a plain-text or selectable-text test. One recruiter also says repeated manual correction is a warning that parsing is poor.
**Evidence verdict: Useful check.** Lever recommends the selectable-text test. A clean copy is reassuring, but it cannot prove every employer system will parse the same way.

### 9. Keep it concise and weight recent, relevant experience
**Video consensus: 8/20** [####------]
Recommendations vary: one page, two pages, and two to three pages. Relevance is the common thread. In the UK, one or two pages is a safer default.
**Evidence verdict: Mixed.** Length is a human-reading convention, not an ATS rule. Academic, technical and creative applications may have different expectations.

### 10. Take application questions seriously
**Video consensus: 4/20** [##--------]
Basic eligibility answers can trigger configured rules. Complete the form carefully and honestly. A polished CV does not override contradictory answers.
**Evidence verdict: Supported.** Greenhouse documents auto-reject based on custom question answers. Its standard application review also lets a recruiter advance or reject manually.

## The most surprising insight: a bad parse is not a rejection
A CV is parsed into fields, a recruiter can search or review candidates, optional matching may rank evidence, and employer rules can reject answers to application questions. These are not one automatic decision. Greenhouse says an unsuccessful parse may leave the original CV attached while details are entered manually. [Read Greenhouse documentation](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse).

This is the point that most generic articles miss. It also keeps the future guide honest: improve readability and relevance, but never promise a secret ATS shortcut.

## Theme by theme

### How screening actually works | Verdict: Supported
Video finding: 20 videos discuss matching or keywords. Five explicitly warn that an ATS is not an autonomous decision-maker.
Analysis: The strongest mental model is four stages: <b>parse the CV, find relevant profiles, review evidence, apply employer rules</b>. Not every employer uses every stage. Greenhouse documents manual application review and optional question-based auto-reject. Workday documents skills matching and HiredScore comparison against requirements. That varies by product, subscription and configuration.
Qualification: An ATS is a database and workflow tool. Parsing, searching, ranking and automatic rejection are different operations.

### Formatting and layout | Verdict: Supported, Mixed
Video finding: 17 videos recommend simple layouts. 14 mention standard or readable fonts. Three suggest keeping a more creative version for direct human review.
Analysis: Keep the main CV deliberately plain. Put contact details in the document body, not a page header, footer or text box. Replace information carried only by graphics with words. A clearer layout also helps the recruiter scanning the result. Creative portfolios can remain separate links where relevant.
Qualification: Use one column, real text, clear headings, a common font and consistent spacing. Columns and tables can corrupt extraction. They do not automatically trigger rejection in every system.
“One column, top to bottom, standard headings.” Jobscan, 3:52 [Open at this moment](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bon1BvjL7N0&t=232s)

### File type | Verdict: Mixed
Video finding: 10 videos discuss PDF or Word. Four recommend PDF, two recommend Word and four accept both or defer to the employer.
Analysis: A PDF is not automatically unreadable. Greenhouse accepts PDF and DOCX for parsing, Oracle Taleo accepts both, and Lever accepts PDF and Microsoft Word. Lever specifically warns about image files. Workday has different parsers across features, including a documented feature that handles DOC, DOCX, HTM and TXT. The practical answer is: follow the form, use a real text document, and verify the extracted fields.
Qualification: Both text-based PDF and DOCX are widely supported. The employer instruction and actual text layer matter more than dogma.

### Keywords and tailoring | Verdict: Supported, Myth
Video finding: All 20 videos cover job-specific keywords. Six tell viewers to prioritise repeated terms. Seven warn against keyword stuffing. Two explicitly reject hidden white text.
Analysis: Start with essential requirements, then preferred criteria and responsibilities. Repeated terms can indicate priorities to a person, but they are not a guaranteed weighting formula. Use exact product or technical names where truthful, then back them with an example. Taleo supports exact and related-term searches. Lever searches parsed resume content. Modern matching can also compare skills more broadly, so literal copying alone is not the whole task.
Qualification: Use the employer language for skills and experience you genuinely have, in natural context. Repeating a phrase many times is not a reliable universal ranking hack.
“Keyword mapping” describes real work. “Keyword stuffing” adds phrases without evidence. Jeff Su, 3:58 onward [Open at this moment](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N1Fm4JYae1I&t=238s)
Independent check: [Oracle Taleo: keyword search](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/taleo-enterprise/otrcg/c-advancedsearchkeywords.html); [Lever: candidate search](https://help.lever.co/s/article/Searching-the-Database-for-Candidates); [Workday HiredScore: Fit and Gap](https://doc.workday.com/hiredscore/en-us/workday-hiredscore/recruiter-productivity-/concept--fit---gap.html)

### Content for the human reviewer | Verdict: Supported
Video finding: 16 videos favour achievements over duties. 13 recommend verified numbers. Seven recommend ownership verbs. Five say to demonstrate soft skills through evidence.
Analysis: Use a simple structure: action, contribution and result. Lead each role with its strongest relevant bullet. Do not overload every line with numbers or adjectives. Two videos recommend briefly explaining gaps rather than leaving an unexplained story. One recruiter recommends trying a different CV version if results disappoint.
Qualification: Specific, recent, relevant results make the CV easier to assess. Strong verbs help only when they accurately describe your contribution.
“Explain risk on the page, not in the interview.” Farah Sharghi, 2:27 [Open at this moment](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eGmZZFJ-8PY&t=147s)
Independent check: [National Careers Service: CV sections](https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/careers-advice/cv-sections); [University of Bradford: UK CV guidance](https://www.bradford.ac.uk/careers/applications/cv/); [Ladders: clear layouts and context](https://www.theladders.com/static/images/basicSite/pdfs/TheLadders-EyeTracking-StudyC2.pdf)

### Sections, dates and contact details | Verdict: Supported, Mixed
Video finding: 11 videos recommend standard headings. Nine stress clear dates or employer details. Six specify reverse chronological work history. One warns against contact details in headers or footers.
Analysis: Use a clean structure: name and contact details, a short role-relevant introduction if it adds value, work experience, education and skills. Put recent work first. Include month and year dates and use one pattern throughout. Keep your email professional. One video also recommends a professional file name, a small but sensible detail.
Qualification: Conventional labels and consistent chronology reduce ambiguity for both parsers and readers. Whether contact details must be vertical or simply in the body is not consistent across creators.
Greg Langstaff suggests translating an odd internal title in brackets, but warns against inflating it. 4:16 to 4:54 [Open at this moment](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=90jauEbtDto&t=256s)

### Checks before sending | Verdict: Useful check
Video finding: Three videos demonstrate a plain-text or selectable-text test. One recommends checking repeated manual corrections. Two warn that third-party ATS scores are not employer scores.
Analysis: Select and copy the CV text into a plain-text editor. Is the reading order intact? Are headings, dates and contact details present? Then check what the actual application autofills. This is a fault-finding check, not a compatibility certificate. Jobscan deserves credit for stating that its match score is its own analysis, not a secret score from the employer.
Qualification: Use extraction and autofilled fields to find obvious faults. Do not treat a third-party score as a pass mark.
“Not a secret number from the company’s own system.” Jobscan on its scanner, 6:25 [Open at this moment](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bon1BvjL7N0&t=385s)
Independent check: [Lever: selectable-text test](https://help.lever.co/hc/en-us/articles/20087345054749-Understanding-resume-parsing); [Greenhouse: partial parse requires correction](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse)

### Length and local conventions | Verdict: Mixed
Video finding: Eight videos recommend a page limit or recent-experience weighting. Three UK videos explicitly advise removing photos and personal details. Recommendations conflict on one versus two pages.
Analysis: UK guidance supports one or two pages for conventional CVs, with longer forms for some specialist applications. Leave out a photograph, date of birth, age, marital status and nationality unless a particular process genuinely requires otherwise. US sources often say resume and favour tighter one-page advice for early careers. Do not import every US convention into a UK guide.
Qualification: There is no universal page limit or global CV template. Local norms, experience and role matter.
Absolutely Ashika: UK employers usually expect no more than two pages, 1:01 [Open at this moment](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rDNtVyzjiL4&t=61s)

## Risky hacks to avoid

- **White text or hidden job descriptions:** The extracted text can expose the tactic. It is manipulation, not evidence of ability. [Video moment](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i3cfqBDH7i4&t=356s)
- **Repeating keywords until the writing becomes unnatural:** No reliable universal repetition rule exists. It makes the human review worse. [Video moment](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bon1BvjL7N0&t=312s)
- **Inflating a previous job title or adding skills you cannot evidence:** Use a truthful clarification in brackets if needed. Never turn an assistant role into an executive one. [Video moment](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=90jauEbtDto&t=283s)
- **Choosing a template for visual novelty rather than readable text:** Skill bars, icons, text boxes and graphics can obscure or scramble important information. [Video moment](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4_oufjP2yeM&t=608s)
- **Optimising for a scanner number instead of the employer:** A third-party score is only an estimate. Check the actual job and actual application fields. [Video moment](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bon1BvjL7N0&t=385s)
- **Leaving template placeholders or disguised fake data:** Greenhouse says fake company names and similar placeholders can be skipped by its parser. [Greenhouse parser warning](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse)
- **Being careless or dishonest on knockout questions:** Employer-configured questions can trigger auto-reject. Keep CV and form answers consistent. [Video moment](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4_oufjP2yeM&t=134s)
- **Hiding a career gap behind vague dates:** Brief context is better than inviting the recruiter to invent the story. [Video moment](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eGmZZFJ-8PY&t=158s)

## UK-specific notes

- **Length:** one or two pages is a sound conventional UK default. Academic and specialist CVs can be longer. Video advice ranges from one to three pages.
- **Photographs:** leave a photo out for a conventional UK CV unless specifically required.
- **Personal details:** leave out age, date of birth, marital status and nationality. Use a town or broad location where suitable.
- **Right to work:** answer eligibility questions honestly.
- **Overseas qualifications:** provide understandable UK context or equivalence where useful. SCALER recommends explaining equivalence at 7:10, while the University of Bradford gives UK-specific context.
- **References:** these can usually wait until requested, though conventions vary for undergraduate placements.
- UK checks: [National Careers Service](https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/careers-advice/cv-sections) and [University of Bradford](https://www.bradford.ac.uk/careers/applications/cv/).

## How this becomes an InterviewAlpha guide

### Proposed title
**CV screening software: how to get your CV read, parsed and noticed**

### Proposed outline
1. **What an ATS really does:** parsing, search, matching and configurable employer rules.
2. **The two checks every CV needs:** can software extract it and can a person see the fit?
3. **A simple layout that travels well:** one column, standard headings, readable font, consistent dates.
4. **PDF or Word?** Follow the instruction, ensure a real text layer, inspect autofilled fields.
5. **Honest keyword mapping in ten minutes:** requirements first, then real examples.
6. **Write for the human:** relevant achievements, concise bullets, verified outcomes.
7. **The two-minute pre-submit check:** plain text, application fields, links, dates, spelling and eligibility.
8. **UK checklist and myths:** length, photos, white text, the 75% claim and third-party scores.

### Natural product connection
Only one of the 20 videos explicitly advises trying a different version when a CV is not producing results. Readers can [try two CV versions with InterviewAlpha and see which gets more replies](https://interviewalpha.app). No guaranteed interview claim is needed.

Recommended visuals: a four-stage screening diagram, a simple-versus-complex extraction example, a PDF or Word decision tree, and a one-page pre-submit checklist.

## Independent sources

- [Greenhouse: Unsuccessful resume parse](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse). Formatting faults can cause full or partial parse failure. The original resume can remain attached and require manual correction. Includes a 2.5 MB parsing limit.
- [Greenhouse: Supported formats](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/360052218132-Supported-formats-for-resumes-cover-letters-and-other-candidate-uploads). Candidate uploads include DOC, DOCX, PDF, RTF and TXT.
- [Greenhouse: Application rules](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/203105595-Application-rules-overview). Auto-reject is triggered by answers to custom application questions with employer-defined rules.
- [Greenhouse: Application review](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/4401963991707-Application-review-stage). Recruiters can review each application and advance or reject it manually.
- [Greenhouse: AI features](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/33043749845403-Greenhouse-AI-features). Current features include keyword suggestions and an optional talent-matching add-on. Features depend on tier and add-ons.
- [Workday: Resume parsing](https://doc.workday.com/admin-guide/en-us/human-capital-management/recruiting/candidates/set-up-prospects-and-candidates/hdc1552497830785.html). Parsing results vary by format and word order. Images can hurt. Languages and Skills are not autofilled by this feature.
- [Workday: Candidate Skills Match](https://doc.workday.com/admin-guide/en-us/human-capital-management/recruiting/candidates/candidate-skills-match/bmj1604095304483.html). Machine-learning similarity based on candidate and requisition skills. Documents formats and limits. Weighting for required skills is supported.
- [Workday HiredScore: Fit and Gap](https://doc.workday.com/hiredscore/en-us/workday-hiredscore/recruiter-productivity-/concept--fit---gap.html). Compares individual basic and preferred requirements with parsed candidate evidence to help explain a grade.
- [Oracle Taleo: Candidate profiles](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/taleo-enterprise/20b/otfru/candidate-profiles.html). Accepts Word and PDF among other formats when creating a profile from a resume.
- [Oracle Taleo: Candidate keyword search](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/taleo-enterprise/otrcg/c-advancedsearchkeywords.html). Supports keyword, exact, related and conceptual search across candidate information and supported attachments.
- [Lever: Understanding resume parsing](https://help.lever.co/hc/en-us/articles/20087345054749-Understanding-resume-parsing). Accepts PDF and Microsoft Word. Cannot parse JPG or PNG. Recommends the selectable-text check. Read through search result snippets.
- [Lever: Searching the candidate database](https://help.lever.co/s/article/Searching-the-Database-for-Candidates). Searches active and archived candidates and parseable resume content. Read through search result snippets.
- [Harvard Business School: Hidden Workers research summary](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/how-to-tap-the-talent-automated-hr-platforms-miss). Survey included 8,720 workers and 2,275 executives in the US, UK and Germany. Finds rigid filtering can exclude viable candidates. It does not prove a universal 75% rejection rate.
- [Ladders: 2018 eye-tracking study](https://www.theladders.com/static/images/basicSite/pdfs/TheLadders-EyeTracking-StudyC2.pdf). Reports 7.4 seconds for an average initial human screen. Public methodology lacks sample detail, so use as limited evidence, not a law.
- [Computerworld: 2012 ATS article](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1458323/5-insider-secrets-for-beating-applicant-tracking-systems.html). Attributes the 75% claim to Preptel. Useful for tracing the claim, not for treating it as independent validation.
- [CNBC Make It: Recruiter debunks white fonting](https://www.cnbc.com/make-it/2023/09/26/tiktok-white-font-resume-trend-drives-recruiter-nuts-its-not-going-to-work.html). Recruiter explains that hidden words can be extracted and seen, creating a credibility problem.
- [National Careers Service: How to write a CV](https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/careers-advice/cv-sections). UK guidance on tailoring, clear fonts, sections and omitting age, birth date, marital status and nationality.
- [University of Bradford: UK CV guidance](https://www.bradford.ac.uk/careers/applications/cv/). Supports one or two pages for conventional UK CVs, no photo, relevant content and simple ATS formatting.

## Method and limitations
Advice was manually coded from complete timestamped transcripts. Counts reflect a mention of the idea, not endorsement of every stronger claim. Vendor documents establish capabilities, not every employer configuration. Lever pages were verified through indexed help-centre content because direct fetch returned 401 in this environment; all other primary sources were read directly. Product features change. Popularity was not treated as accuracy. This is editorial research, not a promise of interview outcomes or a substitute for an employer instruction.
