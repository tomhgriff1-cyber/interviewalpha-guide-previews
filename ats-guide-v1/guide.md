# How to get your CV past automated screening software

**Estimated reading time: 11 minutes**

If you are worried that a robot will reject your CV before anyone sees it, start with a less frightening fact: a failed CV parse is not the same as an application rejection. Screening software often reads your document into fields such as your name, work history and skills. If that goes wrong, your original CV can remain attached for a person to read, with missing details entered or corrected by hand. [Greenhouse explains this distinction in its parsing guidance.](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse)

That does not mean formatting is unimportant. It means your task is practical rather than mysterious: make the document easy to extract, show that you fit the role, and give the person reviewing it useful evidence. You do not need a secret hack.

## What CV screening software actually does

An applicant tracking system, usually shortened to ATS, is a database and a recruitment workflow. Depending on the employer and its setup, it may:

1. **Parse your CV.** It turns the document into searchable fields such as employers, dates and job titles.
2. **Help recruiters search or match candidates.** This can involve keywords, skills or a comparison with the requirements for a particular vacancy. [Oracle describes exact and related-term searches in Taleo](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/taleo-enterprise/otrcg/c-advancedsearchkeywords.html), while [Workday describes skills matching](https://doc.workday.com/admin-guide/en-us/human-capital-management/recruiting/candidates/candidate-skills-match/bmj1604095304483.html).
3. **Present applications for human review.** Greenhouse, for example, documents a stage where recruiters can advance or reject applications themselves. [See its application review guidance](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/4401963991707-Application-review-stage).
4. **Apply rules to application answers.** An employer can configure automatic rejection based on answers to custom questions. [Greenhouse documents these application rules](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/203105595-Application-rules-overview).

Not every employer uses all of them. No single screening machine makes the same decision everywhere.

## Four reassuring facts that cut through the noise

### A failed parse is not an automatic rejection

If a parser cannot read your CV cleanly, the result may be blank or jumbled fields, not an instant rejection. Greenhouse says an unsuccessfully parsed CV can remain attached while missing information is dealt with manually. That is one system's behaviour, not a promise about every employer, but it disproves the idea that a formatting fault always sends your application into a void. [Read the Greenhouse explanation](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse).

A clean CV still matters. It reduces friction, makes your information easier to find, and avoids relying on a recruiter to repair the application.

### There is no reliable universal "75% rejected by robots" figure

The claim that 75% of CVs, or 75% of qualified applicants, are rejected by an ATS is repeated widely. The research behind this guide did not find a representative published study supporting it. A [2012 Computerworld article](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1458323/5-insider-secrets-for-beating-applicant-tracking-systems.html) traces a related figure to Preptel, a company selling help with applicant tracking systems. A supplier claim is not a universal measurement.

A better-supported concern comes from [Harvard Business School's Hidden Workers research summary](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/how-to-tap-the-talent-automated-hr-platforms-miss): rigid filtering can exclude viable candidates. That is a real problem. It does not prove that three quarters of applications are automatically discarded.

### PDF is not banned, and it is not always best either

Neither absolute claim holds up: "Never send a PDF" and "PDF is always safest".

Greenhouse, Oracle Taleo and Lever accept text-based PDF and Word documents in relevant parts of their products. Lever warns that it cannot parse image files such as JPG or PNG. Workday's supported formats differ between features, which is another reason to follow the instructions on the actual application. See the documentation from [Greenhouse](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/360052218132-Supported-formats-for-resumes-cover-letters-and-other-candidate-uploads), [Taleo](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/taleo-enterprise/20b/otfru/candidate-profiles.html), [Lever](https://help.lever.co/hc/en-us/articles/20087345054749-Understanding-resume-parsing) and [Workday](https://doc.workday.com/admin-guide/en-us/human-capital-management/recruiting/candidates/candidate-skills-match/bmj1604095304483.html).

The sensible choice is straightforward:

- If the employer asks for a format, use it.
- Otherwise, a simple text-based PDF or DOCX can work. Choose the one that survives the plain-text test below.
- Do not send a photograph of your CV, a scan with no usable text layer, or a design exported as one large image.
- Check what the form extracts before you submit it.

Four reviewed videos favoured PDF, two favoured Word and four accepted both or deferred to the employer. There is no universal winner.

### Hiding keywords in white text can backfire

Copying the whole job advert into your CV and colouring it white does not demonstrate that you can do the job. Extracted text can expose the hidden words to a recruiter, making an already doubtful tactic look manipulative. [CNBC Make It reported a recruiter explaining precisely this problem](https://www.cnbc.com/make-it/2023/09/26/tiktok-white-font-resume-trend-drives-recruiter-nuts-its-not-going-to-work.html).

Two reviewed videos explicitly reject the tactic, including [Mary Strange's UK CV video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i3cfqBDH7i4&t=356s). Use important terms visibly and honestly, attached to things you have actually done.

## A practical checklist for an ATS-friendly CV

Think of this as two checks happening together. Can software extract the document? Can a person quickly see why you are worth interviewing? A pretty template cannot answer the second question for you, and a keyword list cannot answer it either.

### 1. Begin with the job, not a generic template

Read the essential requirements first. Then look at desirable criteria and the main responsibilities. Select the experience on your CV that provides evidence against them.

Tailoring does not mean rewriting your entire history for every vacancy. It means choosing and framing relevant truths. If the advert names a system, qualification or technical skill you have used, use its proper name where it naturally belongs. If you have not used it, do not add it to fill a gap.

All 20 videos in the research pack discuss tailoring or job-specific keywords. The [National Careers Service also recommends tailoring your CV to the job](https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/careers-advice/cv-sections). [Jeff Su's explanation of keyword mapping and keyword stuffing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N1Fm4JYae1I&t=238s) captures the distinction well.

Repeated words can help you spot priorities, but no dependable repetition rule exists. Taleo supports exact and related searches; Workday can compare skills more broadly. Aim for evidence, not a keyword quota.

### 2. Use a simple layout with real text

A single-column document, read from top to bottom, is the safest starting point. Keep important information out of graphics, icons, skill-rating bars, text boxes, headers and footers. Greenhouse lists these as potential parsing problems. Workday says parsing varies with format and word order, and images can harm the result. See [Greenhouse's parsing advice](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse) and [Workday's guidance](https://doc.workday.com/admin-guide/en-us/human-capital-management/recruiting/candidates/set-up-prospects-and-candidates/hdc1552497830785.html).

Simple does not mean ugly. Use a common readable font, sensible spacing and clear hierarchy. Bold a job title rather than enclosing it in a graphic. Link to a creative portfolio separately.

Seventeen reviewed videos recommend simple layouts. [Jobscan demonstrates the one-column approach](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bon1BvjL7N0&t=232s). The key warning is about scrambled or missing extraction, not automatic rejection for every table or column.

### 3. Use familiar headings and a consistent chronology

"Work experience", "Education" and "Skills" are easier to recognise than invented section titles. Put your name and contact details in the document body. Use a professional email address and check any portfolio or profile links.

List recent work first. Give each role a clear employer, job title and date range, using the same month-and-year pattern throughout. If your internal title was unusual, you can clarify it in brackets without inflating it. "Customer assistant (checkout and service team)" is honest. "Customer experience director" is not, unless that was your actual role. [Greg Langstaff discusses this distinction](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=90jauEbtDto&t=256s).

A sensible basic order is:

- Name and contact details
- A short introduction, only if it adds useful role-specific context
- Work experience, with recent roles first
- Education and relevant training
- Skills, with evidence elsewhere in the CV

Remove template instructions, fake employer names and placeholder text before sending. Greenhouse notes that fake placeholder data can be skipped during parsing.

### 4. Give the human reviewer evidence, not adjectives

"Hard-working team player" claims a quality. "Resolved a backlog of customer queries while maintaining the team's service target" gives a reader something to assess. The strongest bullet points explain what you did, your contribution and the result.

Use numbers where they are genuine and useful: a budget, a response time, an accuracy improvement, the number of people supported or a measurable service outcome. Not every useful achievement has a neat percentage. Do not invent one to satisfy a formula.

Sixteen reviewed videos favour achievements over lists of duties, and 13 recommend verified numbers. [Farah Sharghi's recruiter guidance](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eGmZZFJ-8PY&t=147s) is particularly helpful on addressing possible concerns on the page. Explain a short employment gap briefly and neutrally if it needs context. Do not hide it behind vague dates and leave a recruiter to guess.

Prioritise recent, relevant material. Projects and volunteering can provide evidence when formal experience is limited.

## Try this simple self-test before you apply

You need no special scanner for this first check. Lever recommends checking that PDF text is selectable; copying into a plain-text editor takes the idea a little further. It is a fault-finding exercise, not a guarantee that every parser will behave identically.

### The plain-text paste test

1. Open the exact file you plan to upload. If it is a PDF, check that you can select the words with your cursor.
2. Select all the text and copy it.
3. Paste it into a plain-text editor, such as Notepad or TextEdit in plain-text mode.
4. Read it from top to bottom without fixing anything.
5. Ask whether a stranger could reconstruct your basic history from the pasted text.

Look for:

- **Missing details:** your name, email, job titles or qualifications have disappeared.
- **Broken reading order:** a later job appears before an earlier one, or two columns have been merged into nonsense.
- **Lost dates:** roles are present but their dates are detached or unclear.
- **Unexplained symbols:** icons have replaced important words.
- **Fragmented words:** odd spacing makes names of systems or qualifications hard to recognise.

If you find a fault, return to the source document. Simplify the relevant layout, move contact details into the body, replace graphics with words, and export again. Then repeat the test.

Finally, inspect the employer's application form. If it autofills your roles, dates or contact details incorrectly, correct those fields. Check the answers to eligibility and custom questions as carefully as the CV. The file and the form should tell the same truthful story.

A third-party scanner may find missing language, but its score is not the employer's hidden pass mark. [Jobscan makes this limitation clear in its own walkthrough](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bon1BvjL7N0&t=385s). Treat such a score as one piece of feedback, not a verdict.

## UK CV conventions: what to include and leave out

Technical readability and local CV conventions are different subjects. Advice from US videos does not always transfer neatly.

**Length.** One or two pages is a sound default for a conventional UK CV. There is no ATS rule requiring one page. Academic and specialist applications can differ. Relevance matters more than an arbitrary page count. The [University of Bradford's UK guidance](https://www.bradford.ac.uk/careers/applications/cv/) supports this approach.

**Photograph.** Leave it out for a conventional UK CV unless the particular process requires otherwise. A photo is not an ATS improvement.

**Personal details.** The National Careers Service recommends leaving out your age, date of birth, marital status and nationality. A town or broad location is usually enough instead of a full street address. See the [National Careers Service guidance](https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/careers-advice/cv-sections).

**Right to work.** Answer the employer's questions honestly. Adding your nationality to the CV is not a substitute for its right-to-work process.

**Overseas qualifications.** Give helpful UK context or an established equivalence where relevant, so a reader does not have to guess what your qualification means.

**References.** These can usually wait until they are requested, although conventions can differ for undergraduate placements. Do not publish someone else's contact details without a good reason.

## Your copyable pre-submit checklist

Copy this list into your notes, or print the checklist section of this page. Use it for the final check, not as a reason to delay an otherwise good application.

```text
CV SCREENING AND APPLICATION CHECKLIST

[ ] I have read the essential and desirable criteria for this specific job.
[ ] My CV gives truthful, relevant evidence against those criteria.
[ ] Important skills and systems are named in natural sentences, not stuffed into a list.
[ ] The document uses a simple reading order, familiar headings and a readable font.
[ ] Important information is not trapped in images, text boxes, headers or footers.
[ ] Employer names, job titles and month-and-year dates are clear and consistent.
[ ] Relevant achievements explain my contribution and, where genuine, its outcome.
[ ] There are no hidden keywords, inflated titles, unsupported skills or template placeholders.
[ ] I have followed the employer's requested file format.
[ ] My PDF or DOCX contains selectable, correctly ordered text, not only an image.
[ ] I have completed the plain-text paste test and fixed any obvious extraction problems.
[ ] The application form's autofilled fields and custom answers match my CV.
[ ] My links work, my contact details are current and the file name is professional.
[ ] I have proofread the file I am actually sending.
[ ] For a conventional UK CV, I have checked length, photo and personal details against current guidance.
```

## Learn from replies rather than chasing a perfect score

No perfect CV will pass every employer's process. Different systems, vacancy criteria and human judgements sit behind each application. Remove avoidable friction, present relevant evidence and pay attention to what happens next.

If one version earns more replies, that is useful information. Compare like with like where possible, and treat a small number of applications as an early indication rather than proof.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is an ATS-friendly CV?

An ATS-friendly CV is a document whose text and structure can be extracted reliably, while making your suitability clear to a human reviewer. It normally uses familiar headings, consistent dates, relevant skills and evidence of your work. There is no universal format that guarantees progression.

### Is PDF or Word better for an ATS?

Follow the employer's instructions. Where both are accepted, a simple text-based PDF or DOCX can work. Greenhouse, Taleo and Lever support both in relevant product documentation. Avoid image-only documents and check the extracted application fields before submitting.

### Can ATS software read tables and columns?

Complex layouts increase the risk of jumbled reading order or missing fields. Greenhouse identifies tables, columns and other complex elements as potential parsing problems. A single column is the simplest, most dependable starting point.

### Do ATS systems automatically reject CVs?

An employer can configure rules based on answers to application questions. Other workflows include manual review, search and optional matching. A parser struggling with your document is not itself the same as automatic rejection. The setup depends on the employer and product.

### How do I test my CV for ATS compatibility for free?

Open the file, confirm that its words are selectable, then copy everything into a plain-text editor. Check the reading order, headings, dates and contact details. Review any autofilled fields in the real application form too. This finds common extraction faults, but cannot test every employer system.

### How long should a UK CV be?

One or two pages is a sensible default for a conventional UK CV, with different expectations for some academic or specialist applications. Focus on recent, relevant evidence rather than fitting an absolute rule. There is no universal ATS one-page requirement.
